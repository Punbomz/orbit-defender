# Orbit Defender project guide

Read this file before changing the project. Keep it updated when core gameplay contracts, storage keys, or page boundaries change.

## Project shape

- This is a static browser game. It uses HTML, CSS, vanilla JavaScript, Canvas, and local assets; there is no build step or package manager.
- `index.html`, `menu.css`, `menu.js`: cinematic launch screen, campaign route, Hall of Aces leaderboard, and the preflight modal. Open the sound and resume/restart choices in a menu modal before navigating so menu music continues while the player chooses; pass the selected sound and resume state into `game.html`. Keep mode, unit, difficulty, launch, and options controls outside the framed planet route; render locked planets as black silhouettes in both modes. Story Campaign follows the unlocked route. Level Practice only allows selecting stages up to the highest unlocked campaign stage. Daily Challenge is a distinct title-screen entry point that deterministically selects a daily stage from the campaign stages unlocked on the local calendar date, passes that date in the `daily` query parameter, and preserves campaign records. Endless unlocks only after defeating the campaign boss; it enters continuous orbital defense at once, uses `planet-degree-overlord.webp`, and increases enemy pressure without changing campaign route progress. Keep the menu scrollable when the viewport cannot fit every control. Use `ui-button-small.png` for compact choice controls and `ui-threat-card.png` for difficulty cards. The menu body background uses space art distinct from the banner so the same planet is not repeated. `assets/favicon.svg` is the game logomark favicon for all pages.
- `game.html`, `style.css`, `game.js`: campaign, tutorials, orbital combat, HUD, and minigame host iframe.
- The flight host shows a single audio toggle and a resume-or-restart choice for saved campaign levels above Level 1; Level 1 starts fresh without a resume prompt. A portrait-orientation prompt appears whenever the viewport is not landscape and covers iframe minigames.
- `trigo_combat.html`, `wiring.html`, `radar.html`, `matching.html`, `satellite_radar.html`, `trigo_merge.html`: standalone minigames and iframe child pages. All use `assets/minigame-tutorial.css` and `assets/minigame-tutorial.js` to follow the campaign lesson composition: metal console frame, blurred stage planet, Astra portrait beside the rounded speech panel on the left, and a game-specific example on the right instead of the unit-circle visual. Preserve each game's existing start/result message contract when editing its tutorial.
- `assets/`: game art and audio-related visuals. The menu route uses one standalone transparent WebP planet per campaign stop (`planet-helios-outpost.webp`, `planet-mirror-belt.webp`, and `planet-shattered-meridian.webp`; `planet-degree-overlord.webp` and `planet-special-angle.webp` are retained art assets and not route destinations. Keep each route asset replaceable without changing route structure. The final boss destination is the Null Armada command base: `nox-command-base-menu.png` is its transparent colored route art, shown as a silhouette while locked; `nox-command-base.png` is its cinematic background, and `nox-flagship.png` is NOX's human-commanded in-combat flagship portrait. Use the base in the boss arrival/departure cinematic, story, lesson, mission art, and route. Boss combat has no planet or station object at the player center; the command base fills the background, while shield weakpoints connect Degree Core power to the unit circle. `ui-button-small.png` is an original transparent compact sci-fi button frame; `ui-threat-card.png` is an original transparent frame sized for Threat Level cards. `ui-console-frame.png` and `dialog-frame.png` are shared metallic cyan console borders; `orbit-interface.css` applies the Story2 PDF art direction to Story, Academy, combat, and all iframe minigames. `favicon.svg` uses the Orbit Defender orbital-ring mark. `visual-novel-character-trio.png` is original generated RGBA art with transparent background for NOVA (male pilot), ASTRA (ship AI), and NOX. `minigame-intercept-intro.webp` and `minigame-intercept-outro.webp` are character-free cinematic backgrounds used by `trigo_combat.html`; their matching PNG files are high-resolution source masters. `matching-navigation-intro.webp` and `matching-navigation-outro.webp` are character-free coordinate-recovery scenes owned by `matching.html`, with matching PNG source masters. `light-path-intro.webp` and `light-path-outro.webp` are character-free Light Path story backgrounds: a broken reflector signal route and its successful three-reflector restoration. Keep dialogue text in HTML, never embedded in images.
- `story-helios-outpost-space.png`, `story-mirror-belt-space.png`, and `story-shattered-meridian-space.png` are character-free full-screen space-and-planet story backdrops for Helios Outpost, Mirror Belt, and Shattered Meridian respectively; keep the established right-side planet and left-side dialogue composition consistent across all three.
- `README.md`: player-facing overview. `AGENTS.md` is the working reference for future code changes.

## Intended campaign loop

Campaign order is Level 1 (Q1 basics) → Level 2 (Q2 reflection) → Level 3 (Q3 and Q4 coordinates) → final boss. Opening story establishes NOVA, ASTRA, and NOX's route-control motive; successful boss defeat plays the campaign ending dialogue before the result summary. Minigame transfer dialogue must stay destination-neutral because one of five minigames is selected randomly after each regular level.

The campaign opens with the STARFALL story and faction introductions, then immediately plays the Level 1 arrival cinematic before the Helios Outpost level story. Each regular level follows: ship arrival cinematic → level story scene → a unified sentence-by-sentence Astra lesson with a unit-circle visual, previous/next controls, and a hands-on weapon drill for each lesson step → defend the circular planet through three waves → brief pause and story entrance transition → after-action story → one randomly selected minigame not yet played in this campaign and its story → ship departure and arrival cinematics → next level story. Orbital Intercept (`trigo_combat.html`) and Card Matching (`matching.html`) own their illustrated intro and result scenes; each iframe reports its result only after the player advances from the ending scene. The host handles campaign travel after that result. Other minigames retain their existing host dialogue story flow. The final boss has its own level story and briefing, then enters three-phase defense without a tutorial, and ends with the campaign summary. Keep the minigame selection list extensible as more minigames are added. Menu Level Practice uses a ship arrival cinematic and the selected level story; regular levels show their unified lesson and drills, while the boss proceeds from story to briefing before the no-progression flight. The route has three regular levels followed immediately by the final boss.

Regular wave quotas are 8, 12, and 16 enemies (36 per level). Each later wave introduces an additional enemy type and raises its starting speed; enemies accelerate during a wave. The wave transition resets speed for the new wave while raising its starting baseline. Do not advance a level until its required wave enemies are cleared.

Enemy prompt intent: `angle-seeker` shows both x and y and asks for θ; `x-seeker` asks for x; `y-seeker` asks for y. Randomly choose each enemy's angle from the stage's taught angles and place it on that same bearing so its location teaches the unit circle. Enemy kinds differ only by question and distinct sprite art; keep movement, speed rules, health, and collision behavior shared. Preserve `x = cos θ`, `y = sin θ`, including signs and reflected angles. Avoid random camera jitter or per-kind position changes that can make enemies appear to blink.

Weapon buttons are always ordered into fixed Quadrant I, II, III, IV, and Axes sections; do not shuffle them. Hide the left weapon rack; answer options appear in the centered target popup. The game has no Academy/teaching launch mode and no 1–9, Shift, E, or H gameplay shortcuts; Space opens the answer popup for the aligned target. Clicking an aligned enemy opens its answer popup; clicking elsewhere on the play field never fires. Dragging on the field or using the mouse wheel previews an Angle Cannon bearing without firing or highlighting angle buttons; choosing an angle answer fires once along the current facing, with no persistent answer highlight. Alt-drag pans the playfield; fixed zoom controls change its scale and reset its view. Target lock follows the player's facing: lock the nearest enemy on the same bearing regardless of weapon selection, and leave the target unlocked when no enemy is aligned. An optional click can lock another enemy only while it shares the current bearing; clear that manual lock when the ship turns away or the enemy is defeated or passes the defense line. The ship's facing never selects an answer or fires automatically; players choose each answer explicitly in the popup. Show each active question only on its enemy or boss label and hide the fixed target banner; explain these controls in the UI. Shields drop randomly when enemies are defeated, are collected at the core, and can be stored up to three; each charge automatically blocks one incoming hit. Show the shield meter in the top HUD. The final boss combines the learned angle and coordinate concepts. Boss HP is represented by fifteen successful weakpoint hits over three phases, five per phase. The phases include unit-circle defense, combo-based energy reload, and synthesis across the learned content. Victory requires reducing boss HP to zero.

Cadet, Pilot, and Ace must remain distinct in enemy speed, player durability, firing cadence/charge recovery, and repeat-miss hint thresholds. Hint mistakes and progression are tracked separately for each enemy instance. Each difficulty threshold (currently 1, 3, and 5 mistakes) advances one of three hint stages: concept, quadrant/reference-angle guidance, then the exact answer. The final stage must reveal the answer. The final boss has three phases of five weakpoint hits each (fifteen total): unit-circle defense, combo-based energy recovery, then synthesis with a relocating weakpoint. Boss support enemies can fire at the player and all phases draw the dedicated transparent Overlord portrait. The right telemetry sidebar, Unit Circle Reference panel, Learning Goal panel, and bottom control/status bar are intentionally absent. The Unit Circle Reference panel is intentionally removed from the gameplay HUD. Do not add it back unless requested. Do not show numeric angle labels around the gameplay orbit.

## Tutorial and speaking dialog

- Every level briefing is editable separately. Persist edits under `orbit-defender-tutorials` as a JSON object keyed by zero-based level index.
- Briefings should remain data-driven so lesson text and images can be revised without changing combat logic.
- The unified Astra lesson always shows every playable angle and its signed (x, y) values on the unit circle; put the current (x, y) readout below the circle, place x = cos θ / y = sin θ horizontally above it, show angle labels inside the ring, and signed coordinate labels just outside the ring. Show only the two sign symbols (for example, +, +) inside each unlocked quadrant; omit x/y prefixes. Align the lesson heading and details to the left edge of the state bar. The show/hide control appears only during hands-on shooting drills. Show those reference labels during the hands-on shooting screen as well, with its top HUD toggle controlling visibility. Draw the planet using the current level art and keep the training enemy visibly rendered. Keep the lesson and randomized coverage drills open-ended: reveal the battle-ready action after every playable angle and distinct coordinate value has been practiced once, while allowing more practice before entering combat. If the player has already cleared the selected level, show the ready button during a hands-on drill so they can skip the remaining lesson practice and enter combat. In randomized shooting practice, spawn targets from beyond the screen edge and move them inward like live enemies; when they reach the core, remove them without reducing player health. Keep animated orbit elements and combat motion active during live defense. The unified Astra lesson is intentionally silent: show one text sentence at a time and animate Astra while it is displayed. Keep the lesson readable without speech APIs. Do not add glow to her portrait; use a rounded speech bubble, a transparent unit-circle diagram, and diagram motion tied to each sentence (radius, coordinates, or target point).
- Story dialogue includes home and history controls above the dialogue panel; history opens in a popup. Clicks on dialogue or scene background advance/finish the current line, while these controls remain interactive.
- Story dialogue uses original generated character art (do not crop illustrations from the reference PDF). Show NOVA as a male pilot, ASTRA as the ship AI, and NOX as the antagonist. Keep all portraits hidden until that character's first spoken line, then reveal and animate them; narration keeps every character dim.
- Keep the visual treatment ready for replaceable art assets; avoid baking dialogue text into images.
- The unified Academy lesson and drill screen for regular levels teaches from a beginner's starting point: angle origin and direction, unit-circle coordinates, x = cos θ / y = sin θ, quadrant signs, and angle-to-coordinate matching. Compose its controls directly on the main `#overlay` screen with the current planet art blurred behind them; do not place them in a nested brief-box window. Present one text sentence at a time in a rounded speech bubble beside Astra, with previous/next controls and a transparent unit-circle visual to the right, then move to its hands-on weapon drill. Put the lesson progress strip in the upper-left and the replay-story button in the upper-right. Begin each drill from the final dialogue button and hide the duplicate outer launch button while the lesson dialogue is active. Show Astra from `character-astra-hologram.png` without distortion, clipping, or added glow; keep lesson text in HTML. Animate the radius, quadrant, coordinate projections, and highlighted point in sync with each displayed sentence. The final boss has no tutorial and proceeds from its story to the boss briefing and defense.
- `game.js` owns the campaign, per-level tutorial drills, post-level stories, and route travel cinematics; `game.html` and `style.css` provide the host dialogue and cinematic layers. Each minigame may own its own story screens in its page; Orbital Intercept and Card Matching show their child-page intro before play and result before reporting to the host. Keep Story, learning, tutorial, orbital defense, and iframe minigames within the common cyan metal console direction defined in `assets/orbit-interface.css`, following the Story2 PDF reference. Stage arrival must finish before its story; after-action stories lead into the selected minigame; its ending story must finish before departure and arrival to the next planet.

## Minigame page contract

- The host selects from the `miniGames` list in `game.js` and loads a separate page in `#zombie-game`.
- The available list includes Orbital Intercept, Wiring, Radar, Card Matching, Satellite Alignment, Trigo Merge, and Light Path; Satellite Alignment asks five coordinate-to-angle questions drawn only from angles taught by completed levels, using signed coordinates and the selected degree/radian unit; a level randomly selects one. Trigo Merge combines equal-angle tiles by addition to create a randomly chosen taught-angle target, sets a move limit by target angle (30°: 2; 60°: 3; 120°: 8; 240°: 14), and displays degree/radian equivalents plus the live move count. Card Matching (`matching.html`) creates five randomized sine, cosine, or tangent question-answer pairs using only angles from completed levels (`level` is one-based), honors the selected degree/radian unit, and succeeds only when all pairs are matched. It shows an illustrated Thai story before play and an outcome story after completion or timeout; the player advances from that story before the host records the result. Its 120-second timer starts after the intro, and the host shares the same timeout. Orbital Intercept has a short mission intro and no setup controls. Each run spawns exactly five enemy ships; player has three HP and wins only by clearing all five before they breach. Selecting a ship opens its assigned trig problem; a correct answer fires a projectile. Its question pool uses only completed-level content, based on one-based `level` (`1` = Quadrant I, `2` adds Quadrant II, `3+` allows Quadrants III and IV). Wiring shows a scenario and instructions before play. Its repair round presents five randomized question wires from the completed level's content, including its taught mix of sine, cosine, coordinates, and quadrant signs, with the selected degree/radian unit. It selects three distinct answer sockets from those questions. Players directly connect matching wires to all three sockets within 30 seconds; each correct connection restores about 33% power, and all three are required to pass. The System Restore tube reports three repaired sockets. Radar asks players to drag five targets using X/Y coordinates; each target has a 60-second timer, and expired targets count as misses. The host allows 300 seconds for the five-target Radar run. It includes a basic arithmetic calculator and per-target X/Y scratch fields, while players recall sine and cosine values themselves. Lesson angles follow the completed level's content and unit. Light Path (`light_path.html`) presents a Thai communication-recovery story and a short hands-on tutorial, then asks players to rotate a beam on the unit circle until the displayed sine or cosine target is aligned; reveals Reflectors A, B, and C one at a time; each station and its target appear only after the previous station is aligned and the beam animation reaches it, preventing the player from previewing later answers. The selected angle shapes the animated beam traveling between stations. It supports pointer/touch and arrow-key angle adjustment, shows live angle/sine/cosine values, accepts a ±3° tolerance, honors the level's taught angle bank and degree/radian display, allows reset, and ends with a result story before reporting to the host. The iframe and host both use a 60-second limit. Players adjust the beam freely and the game checks alignment only when Lock Angle is pressed; a wrong lock attempt plays a mismatch animation and removes five seconds from both child and host timers. Reset returns to Reflector A without replenishing time.
- Pass `autostart=1`, one-based `level`, `unit`, and a level `content` identifier in the child page URL. The child must use these values to choose relevant lesson material.
- Main menu game routes use `mode=campaign` or `mode=practice`; Level Practice also passes one-based `level` to `game.html`. Keep these host launch settings separate from the child minigame URL and result contract.
- When a minigame ends, the child posts `{ type: 'orbit-minigame-result', success, score, level, quizAccuracy }` to its parent. A child-owned result story may first post `{ type: 'orbit-minigame-story-wait' }` to pause the host timeout until the player continues. The host validates the iframe window source before accepting either message.
- Orbital Intercept passes only when all five enemies are cleared and quiz accuracy is at least 70%. Save unsuccessful runs too; a timeout is a failed result.
- Keep the host responsible for moving to the next level and recording results.

Value Cannon presents each distinct nonnegative magnitude once and uses one shared polarity switch to apply + or − across all quadrants; keep quadrant selection out of its weapon buttons.

## Browser storage

- `orbit-defender-runs`: recent completed run records.
- Completed run records include per-topic attempts and correct answers for angle recovery, cosine/x, sine/y, Quadrant signs, weapon matching, and minigame applications; the post-flight assessment shows each topic's evidence, status, and next practice target. Treat low sample counts as insufficient evidence, not mastery.
- `orbit-defender-last-run`: most recently completed run, used by the result summary; records its outcome so a defeated run cannot offer a stale campaign resume checkpoint.
- `orbit-defender-minigames`: outcomes for each minigame played in the current campaign.
- `orbit-defender-minigame-current`: current/last iframe session state, including timeout handling.
- `orbit-defender-campaign-minigames-used`: minigames already selected in the active campaign, so successive campaign stages do not repeat a minigame; reset when a new campaign starts.
- `orbit-defender-route-unlocked`: highest campaign route stage reached; this is separate from run records.
- Level Practice may select only stage indexes at or below `orbit-defender-route-unlocked`; keep later route planets as silhouettes and prevent pointer, keyboard, and direct URL selection past that limit.
- `orbit-defender-tutorials`: per-level tutorial copy overrides. Keep these customizations when a new campaign clears its result records.
- `orbit-defender-campaign-checkpoint`: live campaign level, unit, and difficulty. Offer to resume from that level or start fresh on next launch; clear it when a campaign ends or the player dies. Practice runs never replace it.
- `orbit-defender-endless-unlocked`: `true` after a campaign boss victory; gates Endless mode in the menu and direct game URL.
- `orbit-defender-endless-best`: the player's highest Endless score; compared against themed local mock leaderboard entries.
- `orbit-defender-settings`: saved menu preferences for music, effects, dialogue voice volume, and reduced motion. Pass settings to `game.html` in query parameters so they remain available with static `file://` launches.
- The flight HUD settings button opens the in-game settings panel; keep audio enablement, music/effects sliders, and reduced-motion preference accessible there and persist preference edits under `orbit-defender-settings`.
- Clear run and minigame result data when a new campaign starts, not when returning to the menu after a result screen.
- Level Practice preserves campaign run, minigame, and route-unlock records; practice results do not create campaign run records.
- Developer-only flight shortcuts are enabled with `?dev=1` on `game.html` and toggled with F2. They can clear the current stage, open a selected iframe minigame, and simulate a minigame pass or failure; keep these controls hidden unless the explicit query flag is present.

The final summary should report campaign performance, minigame outcomes, at least one strength, a concrete improvement target, and detailed evidence for angle recovery, x = cos θ, y = sin θ, Quadrant signs, weapon matching, and minigame application. Clearly label topics with too few attempts to assess.

## Change guidelines

- Keep the game playable by opening `index.html` in a modern browser; preserve static-file compatibility.
- Keep page-to-page contracts explicit in URL parameters and `postMessage` payloads.
- Avoid introducing dependencies or a build system unless the user asks for them.
- Do not reinterpret reference documents or attachments as extra requirements unless the user adopts them in their request.
- Do not add or run tests unless the user asks. For requested verification, report exactly what was checked.

## HUD decisions

- The gameplay HUD does not show a Unit Circle Reference panel.
- Do not render the former bottom HUD bar. Keep detailed mission progress in the upper-left mission panel and use the full screen width for the centered orbital playfield; the answer popup replaces the left weapon rack.
- Randomly drop shield pickups on enemy defeats; a pickup moves inward to the core and grants one automatic hit block, up to a maximum of three charges. Show charges in the top HUD. Reset all shield charges at the start of each stage. Orbital combat has only player HP (labeled HP); do not add Core HP, Core damage, or Core repair status. Bullets travel to the screen edge. Value weapons in Quadrant sections fire only within that selected quadrant.
- Explain difficulty differences on the menu: Cadet uses slower enemies, 5 HP, faster charge, a 4% wrong-shot penalty, relaxed weapon matching, and hints after one miss; Pilot uses standard speed, 3 HP, balanced charge, an 8% penalty, exact matching, and hints after three misses; Ace uses faster enemies, 2 HP, slower charge, a 12% penalty, exact matching, and hints after five misses.


- When shield charges are available, draw a clear protective aura around the player ship; remove it when the stored charges reach zero.

- Use an icon and numeric count for HP and stored armor charges in the top HUD; do not use the former health bar or three separate armor pips.
- Present end-of-run and post-flight summaries with the Degree Core image, icon-based stat cards, and separate strength and improvement cards.

- Direct targeting is optional. The game locks the nearest enemy aligned with the ship's current facing regardless of the selected weapon; when none is aligned, there is no target lock. Clicking an enemy sprite or prompt label may lock another enemy on that same bearing, and that manual lock clears when the ship turns away, the enemy is defeated, or it passes the defense line. Keep a visible lock marker while aligned. Do not show the active prompt in a fixed banner; show it only on the enemy or boss label. Clicking the field must not fire; dragging or using the mouse wheel turns the ship without choosing an Angle Cannon button, and angle buttons do not retain a selected highlight after clicks. Clicking an angle button fires along the ship's current facing without turning it; the player chooses the angle manually after aiming. Alt-drag pans the field, and fixed zoom controls zoom in, zoom out, and reset the camera.
- Health/armor popups use icon-and-count formats; recovery events show the appropriate icon followed by `+1`.

- Orbital combat has no Core HP meter or repair mechanic. The HUD shows player HP as ♥ plus a count, and damage or healing popups use icon and number only.

- Use a cyan precision-reticle cursor over the game field; keep interactive controls visibly clickable.
- All campaign stages and hands-on shooting drills use the answer-first flow: clicking an aligned enemy, training target, or stationary boss opens a centered time-slowed question menu with θ and x/y response categories, then the unlocked angle or value answers. From Level 2 onward, Value choices include a shared +/− switch. The selected answer uses the normal firing, accuracy, and wrong-answer penalty rules. Hide the left weapon rack; aiming remains on the field.


Boss combat pauses at the start of each of its three phases for a story-style dialogue between ASTRA, NOVA, and NOX; ASTRA explains that phase’s tactic, then combat resumes. Set the player to 5 / 5 HP when entering the campaign or practice boss stage at every difficulty; ASTRA explains that the recovered Degree Cores reinforced STARFALL. Before each weakpoint question, the boss visibly flies around the field, then stops at its selected angle and reveals the question; hide the question and prevent boss hits while it is moving. After every successful hit, or after eight seconds without a hit, choose a new question and flight destination; do not repeat the exact previous question consecutively. The boss can move at different radii within the field instead of staying at the orbital edge. Show boss HP as a percentage both beside the boss and in the mission panel.
